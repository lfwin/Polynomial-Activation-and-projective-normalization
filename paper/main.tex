\begin{abstract}
Two ingredients decide whether a deep network trains: the \emph{nonlinearity}
that breaks linearity, and the \emph{normalization} that keeps its signal in
range. This paper studies both, and their interaction, through the lens of a
single construction: polynomial activations paired with \emph{projective}
($\ell_2$) normalization. We take as our starting point a ten-layer
fully-connected network whose activation is the projective polynomial
$f(z) = z + \lambda z^{3}$, whose output is projected onto the unit sphere.
We separate the two mechanisms that this construction entangles --- the
\emph{polynomial nonlinearity} and the \emph{projective normalization} --- and
evaluate each against the activations and normalizers that current large
language models actually use (ReLU, GELU, SiLU, the gated SwiGLU and GeGLU;
BatchNorm, LayerNorm, RMSNorm), plus classical controls (tanh, identity) and
learnable polynomial bases (Legendre, Chebyshev, KAN-style cubic B-splines).
Experiments span MNIST and eight synthetic datasets, depths from 2 to 50
layers, polynomial orders 2--5, $\lambda \in [10^{-3}, 10^{-1}]$, and five
seeds. Three results emerge, all of them about the polynomial--normalization
pair rather than about either alone. First, the polynomial nonlinearity is
\emph{not} competitive on raw accuracy: it trails GELU by $\polyNoneToBest$
points at equal parameter count. Second, the projective normalization recovers
$\polyGap$ of those points with \emph{zero} learned parameters, and it does so
by a mechanism we can measure --- it pins the per-layer activation norm at
unity (\actNormPolyRatio$\times$ its initial value) where ReLU's decays to
\actNormReluRatio$\times$ and a gated unit's collapses to \actNormSwiRatio.
Third, this same normalization is what makes depth reachable: the projected
polynomial tracks ReLU to within \LtenProjVsReluPt\ points at depth ten and
\LthirtyProjVsReluPt\ points at depth thirty, whereas an unnormalized
polynomial collapses to chance at depth \dThirty; and a gated unit without
normalization fails outright while the same unit trains at \gluLayerMean\%
once normalization is added. We report the failure boundary honestly: at depth
\dFifty\ both designs collapse to $\approx$\LfiftyCollapseAcc\%, so projective
normalization buys depth, not unbounded depth.
\end{abstract}
